“5-HT inhibitor” is convenient shorthand, but it is not quite what selective
serotonin reuptake inhibitors do. These compounds inhibit the serotonin
transporter, reducing reuptake of 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT); they do not inhibit
the serotonin molecule itself. This distinction matters when chemical
structures and pharmacological mechanisms are discussed together.

The three examples below are all classified as SSRIs, yet their structures are
visibly different. They also provide a useful test of two common molecular data
paths: connectivity supplied as SMILES and a computed conformer supplied as an
SDF record.

> This is a cheminformatics visualization, not medical guidance. The displayed
> coordinates are not evidence of a bioactive conformation or clinical behavior.

## Fluoxetine: connectivity from SMILES

[Fluoxetine](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Fluoxetine) is represented
here by a SMILES string from its PubChem record. SMILES encodes atoms, bonds, and
stereochemical annotations, but it does not normally carry Cartesian
coordinates. RDKit therefore generates a two-dimensional layout before the
viewer builds its ball-and-stick scene. Explicit hydrogens are added after that
coordinate generation step.

<MoleculeViewer
  smiles="CNCCC(c1ccccc1)Oc1ccc(cc1)C(F)(F)F"
  hydrogens="add"
  viewerHeight="21rem"
  viewPadding={1.05}
  class="my-8"
  ariaLabel="Interactive molecular model of fluoxetine generated from SMILES"
  title="Fluoxetine"
  titleHref="https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Fluoxetine"
  subtitle="C₁₇H₁₈F₃NO"
  sourceLabel="SMILES · PubChem CID 3386"
  sourceHref="https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/3386"
  linkTarget="_blank"
/>

The generated coordinates make connectivity easy to inspect, but rotating the
model also reveals the limitation: every atom begins in a flat depiction. That
is appropriate for a structure diagram, not a claim about a preferred
three-dimensional shape.

## Sertraline: preserving a 3D conformer

[Sertraline](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/sertraline) contains two
defined stereocenters and a compact tetralin framework with a dichlorophenyl
substituent. This viewer starts from PubChem's computed 3D SDF conformer and
preserves the hydrogens already present in that record.

<MoleculeViewer
  sdf={sertralineSdf}
  hydrogens="preserve"
  viewerHeight="23rem"
  viewPadding={1.12}
  class="my-8"
  ariaLabel="Interactive computed 3D conformer of sertraline"
  title="Sertraline"
  titleHref="https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/sertraline"
  subtitle="C₁₇H₁₇Cl₂N"
  sourceLabel="3D conformer · PubChem CID 68617"
  sourceHref="https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/68617"
  linkTarget="_blank"
/>

Unlike the SMILES-derived layout, the SDF supplies x, y, and z coordinates.
Those coordinates describe one computed conformer. Other conformers remain
possible, and a protein-bound geometry would need experimental or modeled
complex data rather than this isolated-molecule record.

## Escitalopram: stereochemistry without explicit hydrogens

[Escitalopram](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Escitalopram) is the
S-enantiomer of citalopram. Its PubChem conformer contains explicit hydrogens,
but this example removes them before rendering so the heavier-atom framework
and defined stereocenter remain visually prominent.

<MoleculeViewer
  sdf={escitalopramSdf}
  hydrogens="remove"
  viewerHeight="22rem"
  viewPadding={1.18}
  class="my-8"
  ariaLabel="Interactive computed 3D conformer of escitalopram without explicit hydrogens"
  title="Escitalopram"
  titleHref="https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Escitalopram"
  subtitle="C₂₀H₂₁FN₂O"
  sourceLabel="3D conformer · PubChem CID 146570"
  sourceHref="https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/146570"
  linkTarget="_blank"
/>

Removing hydrogen atoms changes the visual density, not the molecular identity
encoded by the record. It is often useful for emphasizing a scaffold, although
explicit hydrogens can be important when discussing protonation, hydrogen
bonding, or a particular experimental structure.

## Comparing the representations

<div class="max-w-full overflow-x-auto">

| Compound | PubChem CID | Formula | Viewer input | Hydrogen mode | Structural feature |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fluoxetine | 3386 | C₁₇H₁₈F₃NO | SMILES with generated 2D coordinates | Add | Flexible diaryl ether with a trifluoromethyl group |
| Sertraline | 68617 | C₁₇H₁₇Cl₂N | Computed 3D SDF conformer | Preserve | Tetralin framework with two defined stereocenters |
| Escitalopram | 146570 | C₂₀H₂₁FN₂O | Computed 3D SDF conformer | Remove | S-enantiomer with an isobenzofuran framework and nitrile |

</div>

The shared pharmacological class does not imply a shared scaffold. More
importantly for visualization, the input format determines what the renderer
can honestly show. A SMILES-derived layout demonstrates connectivity; an SDF
conformer adds one possible geometry. Neither representation, by itself,
explains transporter affinity, selectivity, efficacy, or safety.
